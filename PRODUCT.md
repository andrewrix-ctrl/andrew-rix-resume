# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Two readers arrive at this page, and both matter.

Enterprise clients and executives — CIOs, transformation sponsors, procurement — deciding whether to bring Andrew's practice into their organisation. They skim: they want to know quickly whether this person operates at their altitude, and they read the client list before they read the prose.

Hiring managers and recruiters assessing Andrew for a role. They read it as a CV: scanning credentials, checking dates, looking for the shape of a career rather than a pitch.

Neither is a subordinate case. The page has to survive a thirty-second executive skim and a careful line-by-line read of the experience timeline without becoming two different documents.

## Product Purpose

A personal site for Andrew Rix, Fujitsu's AI-Native Practice Lead, that presents his career as a complete and scannable record. Success is a visitor coming away with the full picture — the AI-Native leadership, the SPCT credential, the decade of scaled transformation behind it — without having to ask for a PDF or piece it together from LinkedIn. The experience timeline is the centre of gravity; everything else frames it.

## Positioning

Andrew helps complex organisations evolve faster than their environment. He is the rare practitioner who arrived early to AI and has the scaled-transformation record to make that leadership credible rather than fashionable.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: connect on LinkedIn.
- Secondary CTA: email Andrew.Rix@fujitsu.com — the direct route for a client or executive who would rather write than connect.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: *helps complex organisations evolve faster than their environment.*
- Belief ladder, in order: (1) he is genuinely senior — 25+ years, SPCT-credentialled; (2) he is on the front edge now, not resting on the Agile years; (3) he has done it at real scale, in named organisations; (4) he is someone you would want in the room.
- Proof on hand, already carried by the page: AI-Native Trainer 2026 and SAFe 6 SPCT Verified Expert badges; SPCT scarcity (fewer than 200 worldwide); 750+ practitioners trained at NPS 89; Fujitsu Platinum Achievement Award; Global SAFe Summit presentations (Denver 2022, Nashville 2023); client logo strip covering Fujitsu, Department of Defence, Qantas, Perpetual, Transport for NSW, City of Gold Coast, NSW Family and Community Services, and Fujitsu Uvance.

## Brand Personality

Assured, curious, ahead. Senior and settled, but visibly leaning forward — the person who got to AI early and is comfortable there. The voice states the record plainly and lets scale do the persuading; it never sells. Curiosity is the throughline that connects the Agile decade to the AI one, and it should read as temperament rather than as a claim.

## Anti-references

- **The generic AI landing page.** Gradient orb hero, glass cards, "supercharge your workflow", purple-on-black SaaS template. A page about AI leadership that looks like every AI product launch defeats its own argument.
- **A LinkedIn profile clone.** Endorsement bars, skill pills, a wall of undifferentiated roles. If this reads as a skinned feed profile, there is no reason to visit it instead of the profile.
- **A plain PDF resume on the web.** A document dumped into HTML with no design intent.

## Design Principles

**The record is the argument.** Named organisations, real numbers, dated credentials. Nothing on this page should assert seniority that the timeline does not already demonstrate.

**Practise what the practice claims.** The page is evidence of the AI-Native argument it makes. Craft, performance and restraint are part of the proof; a sloppy page undercuts the copy no matter what the copy says.

**One page, two reading speeds.** The executive skim and the line-by-line CV read are both first-class. Hierarchy carries the skim; the experience timeline rewards the depth read. Do not solve this by splitting into two documents.

**Ahead, not loud.** Being early to AI is shown through what the page does and how current it feels, never through the visual vocabulary of AI marketing.

**Accessibility is part of the credibility.** Defence and government readers are in the audience. A page that fails them is arguing against itself.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA, treated as non-negotiable rather than a target — government and defence clients are among the readers, so accessibility is itself part of the credibility argument. Body text at 4.5:1 or better in both themes, visible focus states, full keyboard navigation, and reduced-motion alternatives for the reveal, count-up and ambient background animations.

Known gap: the `--muted-2` grey (#66798d) used for the small uppercase labels — contact label, client-strip caption, role dates, table headers, footer — measures roughly 3.9:1 against the light-theme background, under the 4.5:1 small-text bar. It clears the bar in dark mode. Fix the light-theme value.
